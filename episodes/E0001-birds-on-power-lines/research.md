# E0001 research ledger

Question: Why don't birds get electrocuted on power lines?

Accessed: 2026-08-10

## Editorial conclusion

A bird perched on a single conductor has both feet—and nearly all of its body—at almost the same electrical potential. That provides almost no voltage difference across the body, so very little current flows through it. The bird can be electrocuted if it simultaneously touches conductors at different potentials, or an energized conductor and grounded hardware, because its body then completes a conducting path.

The script intentionally says “almost” rather than presenting a physical wire as a perfect ideal conductor. It also avoids implying that birds are immune to electricity.

## Primary and institutional sources

- [Harvard Natural Sciences Lecture Demonstrations](https://sciencedemonstrations.fas.harvard.edu/presentations/bird-high-voltage-transmission-line): explains the voltage-difference requirement and contrasts one wire with a neighboring wire or ground.
- [MIT School of Engineering](https://engineering.mit.edu/ask-an-engineer/how-do-birds-sit-on-high-voltage-power-lines-without-getting-electrocuted): explains equal potential at both feet and the danger of touching a second wire or grounded pole.
- [U.S. Geological Survey](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/electrocution): documents real bird electrocutions when appendages bridge energized parts or energized and grounded metal.
- [U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service](https://www.fws.gov/story/avoidance-and-minimization-measures-power-lines): describes electrocution when a bird completes a circuit across energized parts or energized and grounded parts.
