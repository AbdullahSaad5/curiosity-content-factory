# E0002 research ledger

Question: Why are airplane windows rounded?

Accessed: 2026-08-10

## Editorial conclusion

Aircraft windows interrupt the load path in a pressurized fuselage. A sharp corner creates a more severe localized stress concentration because the load must turn abruptly. Rounded edges give that load a smoother path and reduce—not eliminate—the concentration. Repeated pressurization makes fatigue behavior important.

The script treats the de Havilland Comet as an engineering lesson, not as a simplistic claim that window shape was the only factor in its accidents. FAA material also identifies fatigue-testing assumptions and material changes in the test article as important parts of the history.

## Primary and institutional sources

- [FAA Lessons Learned: de Havilland DH-106 Comet 1](https://www.faa.gov/lessons_learned/transport_airplane/accidents/G-ALYV): detailed account of pressurization cycles, fatigue, stress concentrations at squarish window/frame corners, testing limitations, and the later oval-window design.
- [Smithsonian National Air and Space Museum](https://howthingsfly.si.edu/ask-an-explainer/why-are-pressurized-windows-rounded-corners): concise explanation that rounded corners reduce pressurization-related stress concentrations.
- [FAA Lessons Learned: Boeing 707-300](https://www.faa.gov/lessons_learned/transport_airplane/accidents/G-BEBP): defines a stress concentration as a localized stress field around a structural discontinuity.
- [FAA AC 25.775-1](https://www.faa.gov/regulations_policies/advisory_circulars/index.cfm/go/document.information/documentID/22666/1000): active structural-design guidance for windows and mounting structures in pressurized airplane cabins.

## Safety and claims notes

- Do not state that rounded corners remove stress; they reduce severe concentration.
- Do not imply window shape was the sole causal factor in the Comet history.
- Avoid crash imagery, casualty counts, and sensational language.
- Use original diagrams only; no accident photos or third-party aircraft footage.
