# Editorial and monetization policy

## The channel promise

Answer a familiar question in plain English with an original visual explanation that a general US/UK audience can understand without prior knowledge.

The format may be consistent. The substance may not be interchangeable.

## Episode acceptance gate

An episode proceeds only when all of these are true:

- The question is understandable in one sentence and has broad everyday relevance.
- The answer can be responsibly explained in 35–60 seconds.
- At least two credible institutional, government, standards, or primary sources support it.
- Every factual claim maps to a source in the manifest.
- The script is original wording and adds a genuine explanatory model, not a reading of a webpage.
- The visual logic is specific to the question rather than a generic slideshow.
- The script stays at or below an estimated Flesch–Kincaid grade level of 10.5.
- All media is locally generated or supported by written rights evidence.
- The title and thumbnail accurately match the answer.

Reject health treatment, personal finance, legal advice, breaking news, elections, ongoing conflict, graphic harm, celebrity rumors, or claims that cannot be checked efficiently. Those topics add accuracy, safety, disclosure, and advertiser-suitability risks that this lightweight factory is not designed to manage.

## Originality and anti-spam gate

YouTube's current monetization policy requires original, authentic content and says mass-produced, repetitive, or generic AI-template videos may be ineligible. The policy does allow a repeated format when the substance of each video is materially varied and educational. See [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392).

Therefore:

- Never generate several near-identical episodes from one wording template.
- Vary the conceptual diagram, narrative progression, opening, and conclusion.
- Do not publish image slideshows, scrolling text, scraped clips, compilations, or readings of articles.
- Do not use engagement bait, keyword stuffing, fake urgency, or misleading thumbnails.
- Keep research notes, original render source, and rights evidence so authorship is auditable.

Facebook advises posting content you created yourself and its monetization terms require control of all necessary global rights. See [Facebook copyright guidance](https://www.facebook.com/help/308895412492789) and [Content Monetization Terms](https://www.facebook.com/legal/content_monetization_terms).

## Factual standard

- Prefer primary sources; use institutional explainers to translate technical material.
- Preserve uncertainty and qualifiers such as “usually,” “approximately,” and “can.”
- Avoid exact numbers unless the episode needs them and a source directly supports them.
- Never turn correlation into causation.
- If credible sources disagree on the central answer, stop the episode.
- Recheck links and claims immediately before release when the subject can change over time.

## Synthetic-media standard

The current videos use visibly stylized diagrams and a non-imitative local system voice. YouTube says production assistance and unrealistic synthetic visuals generally do not require the altered-content disclosure, while realistic fabricated people, places, or events do. See [YouTube's synthetic-content disclosure guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491).

The manifest still records the production method. If an episode ever depicts a realistic synthetic person, place, event, or someone else's cloned voice, set `realisticSyntheticMedia` to `true` and answer the platform disclosure accordingly.

## Human release gate

Automation may research, draft, render, package, and QA. It may not make an episode public until an authorized person has:

- watched the entire video with sound;
- confirmed every caption and claim;
- reviewed current platform disclosure choices;
- confirmed rights and advertiser suitability;
- approved the title, thumbnail, and scheduled time.
